# Montage: sequencing the moments

A page is an edited film, not a stack of sections. The storyboard fits in a table of 6 to 9 rows:

| Content | Nature | Recipe | What the motion says | Background | Mode | Axis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## 1. The order follows the visitor's questions

Who? → Why you? → What? → The proof → Where and how? → Act.

The middle can be reordered. The first screen always answers "who" (name and trade readable), and the end always gives a way to act.

## 2. Alternation

- **Background:** image, paper (light), night (dark) or a colour field. Two consecutive moments never share a background, except for a short run of reading.
- **Mode:** watch, read, choose, act. Never "watch" twice in a row.
- **Axis:** vertical, zoom (media expand, scale-through), horizontal (gallery), depth (curtain, stacked cards). Change axis at least twice per page.
- **Scale:** alternate wide shots (full screen, giant type) and close shots (body text, lists).

## 3. Density

- One showpiece moment per third of the page, and always a reading moment between two showpieces.
- Two pinned sections per page at most, three on a long page. Their combined scroll distance stays under about four screens.
- The intro lasts 2 to 4 s. It ends exactly on the final layout, and scrolling is released before it ends.

## 4. The signature move

Every site has one moment people remember. Name it in the storyboard and give it the most care. It must not appear in any of the last three sites in `registry.md`.

Examples:
- Test 01: the image window that becomes the full-screen video, then the thumbnail that becomes the practice-area page.
- Other leads: a scale-through into one letter of the name, stacked cards that become a menu, a horizontal gallery that bends.

## 5. Click transitions: always by continuity

Every click that changes the context starts from a visible element and transforms it. Never cut hard.

| Transition | When | Card |
|---|---|---|
| **Shared element**: the thumbnail becomes the detail image | opening an item from a list or a catalogue | 06 |
| **Morphing**: the button becomes the panel | menu, filters, short form | 09 |
| **Overlay with label**: a curtain and a giant word | changing "room" (appointment, booking, next page) | 10 |
| **Curtain or wipe** | full page change | lexicon §D |

On close, play the path backwards: the object returns to where it came from.

## 6. Navigation

- It is fixed, with no background. Its colour follows the section underneath (theme per section, and a computed theme when the background changes during a pin).
- The main call to action and the contact are always visible. On mobile, an action bar appears once the first screen is left.

## 7. Example (do not copy): test 01, a notary office in Cannes

| Content | Nature | Recipe | Background | Mode | Axis |

## 8. Three other possible sequences (to show the range)

- **Restaurant.**
  - The intro is a logo reveal.
  - The promise comes in with a blur-in.
  - The menu is presented as stacked cards (catalogue).
  - The place uses parallax in the frame.
  - Booking is a button that becomes a form.
  - The footer is the giant wordmark.
- **Architect.**
  - The intro is a scale-through into one letter.
  - The projects are a scroll-spy list with a pinned preview, then a shared element to the project page.
  - The process uses pinned steps.
  - The press runs in a marquee.
  - Contact is an overlay with a label.
- **Hotel.**
  - The first screen is a curtain over a video.
  - The manifesto switches style on scroll.
  - The rooms are a horizontal scroll, then a shared element.
  - The seasons use a background colour shift per section.
  - Booking goes through a morphing nav.
